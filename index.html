I am a communication researcher studying how messages, psychological predispositions, and emerging AI technologies shape the way people evaluate information, respond to persuasion, and make decisions.

My dissertation examines conversational AI as a way to build resistance to misinformation. More broadly, my research spans persuasion, misinformation, strategic communication, and AI-mediated communication in health, climate, and science contexts.

I am interested in designing evidence-based and ethically responsible interventions that enhance public understanding, reduce stigma and polarization, and support informed and equitable decision-making.

My published work appears in AI & Society, the Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media, Media, War & Conflict, and the Review of Communication. I also teach courses in artificial intelligence and oral communication.

I hold an M.A. in Mass Communication and a B.A. in Communication and Psychology from Sogang University. In 2024, I was a summer research intern with the World Bank’s eMBeD team.

I’m always happy to connect about research collaborations, ideas, or a good question: spark143@umd.edu.
